EXHIBITION17 – 19 September 2026NIO House Amsterdam, sixth and seventh floor

Maison d'Artiste

An exhibition for GLUE Amsterdam

Where GROUND lays bare the ground a maker's practice stands on, Maison d'Artiste stages what happens when that practice enters a room built for living — a table that is eaten at, a chair that is sat in, a lamp that is switched on.

Before it was anything else, this building was a house of things made by hand and mind. From 1908 until 2012, Metz & Co furnished Amsterdam's interiors here, on the conviction that everyday objects deserve the same attention as art. In 1933 Gerrit Rietveld crowned it with a glass dome: a stage reserved for the design and fashion no one else dared to show. The building has waited, since, to be that again.

Maison d'Artiste does not invent a function for the sixth and seventh floor. It restores one. The sixth floor is one continuous space of 217m², divided into four rooms; the seventh is 76m² beneath Rietveld's dome. You can enter the route from either end.

By day the house receives whoever finds it. By evening, as any house does, it closes its door to all but those it has chosen to let in.

—How the two relate

GROUND and Maison d'Artiste are not one exhibition split across two floors, nor two unrelated events sharing a building. They are two chapters of the same argument, staged within a single route.

Each remains legible on its own: GROUND does not need Maison d'Artiste to make sense, and Maison d'Artiste does not need GROUND. During GLUE they are presented as one walk through the building — from the fourth floor, through the sixth, to the seventh. From ground, to house, to dome.
